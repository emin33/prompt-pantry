# Universal Orlando Butterbeer: A Researched, Kitchen-Tested Recipe Specification (Cold, Frozen, Hot)

## TL;DR
- **The Universal Orlando Butterbeer is a non-alcoholic, dairy-free amber soda base (cream-soda-like, sweetened with real cane sugar, not HFCS) topped with a whey-containing non-dairy whipped foam tasting of butterscotch and shortbread**; the most credible home reproduction is a chilled cream-soda + fresh butterscotch-syrup base finished with a marshmallow-fluff + non-dairy whipped-topping (Dream Whip-style) + butterscotch foam, and that is the formulation specified below for all three variants.
- **The single most authentic foam is the marshmallow-creme + Dream-Whip + butterscotch + butter/caramel/vanilla extract approach** popularized by Todd Wilbur/Top Secret Recipes — it matches the Universal in-park allergen sheet (foam contains whey powder and tartrazine; no soy, egg, peanut, tree nut, fish, shellfish, sesame, sulfite, or gluten) better than a pure heavy-cream Chantilly, and side-by-side park comparisons in fan forums consistently rank it closest to the real thing.
- **For each variant, the master ratios per 12-oz mug are**: Cold = 10 oz cold cream soda + 2 Tbsp fresh butterscotch syrup + ⅛ tsp salt + extracts + ~⅓ cup foam; Frozen = same liquid base churned in an ice-cream maker / Ninja Slushi (or twice-stirred freezer method) for 25–30 min, target Brix ~18–22; Hot = 1 cup whole milk + 2 Tbsp butter + 2 Tbsp brown sugar + 2 Tbsp butterscotch syrup + 2 Tbsp heavy cream steamed to ~160 °F (cream soda omitted or used only as a 2 Tbsp finishing splash).

## Key Findings

### 1. Source & Canon
- **Literary origin.** Butterbeer is first served to Harry in *Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban* (1999) at the Three Broomsticks in Hogsmeade. Rowling describes it in the books only as a hot, foaming or cold-bottled drink that gets house-elves drunk and is faintly warming to humans. The single most cited authorial flavor descriptor is from **Bon Appétit, January 2002**: "I made it up. I imagine it to taste a little bit like less-sickly butterscotch." (Quote attested in the Harry Potter Lexicon and AP wire syndication; Condé Nast does not host pre-2003 *BA* issues online.)
- **Park collaboration.** The theme-park beverage was developed by **Steven Jayson, VP & Corporate Executive Chef, Universal Parks & Resorts**, with Ric Florell. According to Jim Hill's July 2011 *Huffington Post* article, Jayson made a key research trip to Scotland to meet Rowling **in July 2008** and **"labored for nearly three years"** before the June 2010 Wizarding World opening; Hill cites "company insiders" for the figure of roughly 15–16 iterations. *Marketplace* (APM, 4 April 2016) adds the further-sourced detail that **"Butterbeer was tested over 15 times, once by JK Rowling herself."** The final tasting was done in the UK with Rowling, who reportedly chose the right glass from five and said "Yes, Chef. That's it." (originating account: Jim Hill, July 2011).
- **Mandates and constraints (multiply attested):**
  - **No alcohol** (Universal — family-friendly).
  - **No dairy in the base** and **no gluten/nuts** in the base (Universal — allergen accessibility). *Marketplace* (4 April 2016) summarizes: "mandates from Universal — that the beverage not use any allergens, like nuts, dairy, or gluten."
  - **Real cane sugar, not high-fructose corn syrup** (Rowling's stipulation, per Jim Hill's 2011 piece and *Marketplace*).
- **Jayson's flavor intent** (Bon Appétit interview): "We wanted this beverage to be for everyone — it wasn't going to be alcoholic… It had to taste unfamiliar, yet soothing and smooth. I thought of those soft butter cookies, or butterscotch, and that's what the recipe is based off of." Universal's official marketing copy describes the drink as "reminiscent of shortbread and butterscotch." Jayson also told *Bon Appétit*: "A 12-ounce portion of Butterbeer has fewer calories than a 12-ounce can of soda. We were very sensitive to calorie count."
- **Foam composition.** The cold/frozen base has always been dairy-free, but the original foam topping contains **whey powder** and tartrazine (yellow #5) per Universal's in-park allergen sheet (reported by allergy bloggers Alexis's Gluten Free Adventures and The Mouse For Less). It is otherwise free of soy, egg, peanut, tree nut, fish, shellfish, sesame, sulfite, and (per Chef Bob's email confirmation) gluten. The structure of the original foam is therefore best matched by a **non-dairy whipped-topping mix (Dream Whip / Rich's Whip-style)** combined with marshmallow creme, butterscotch syrup, and flavor extracts — not heavy whipping cream. **On 2 March 2023**, Universal launched a fully vegan foam at Three Broomsticks (Hogsmeade) and the Leaky Cauldron (Diagon Alley) per its official press release; the plant base has not been publicly disclosed.
- **Variant differences across parks:**
  - **Universal Orlando** (Hogsmeade at Islands of Adventure, Diagon Alley at USF, plus CityWalk Universal Studios Store as of 2024): cold, frozen, and hot since 2014. **Hot Butterbeer is dairy-based** (its base contains dairy; the foam-free vegan modification is not available for hot). At **Epic Universe (opened 22 May 2025)**, the new "Ministry of Magic" land introduced French-themed "Bièraubeurre"; per WDW News Today's full menu reporting (6 May 2025), the **Bièraubeurre Cart** sells only Bièraubeurre Froide (cold) and Bièraubeurre Glacée (frozen) at $8.99 each — there is no hot version on the cart, though sit-down venues Café L'air De La Sirène and Le Gobelet Noir offer the hot version year-round.
  - **Universal Hollywood:** identical formulation; introduced 2016. Hot Butterbeer added year-round.
  - **Universal Studios Japan:** same beverage, served in disposable cup / plastic souvenir / premium stein tiers.
  - **Warner Bros. Studio Tour London:** sells a different bottled version (vegan/vegetarian/gluten-free, brewed in the UK, MinaLima-designed bottle) and a draft Butterbeer with similar foam; commonly described as slightly less sweet than Universal's.
  - **Three Broomsticks vs. Hog's Head pour:** identical drink poured from the same lines; Hog's Head is generally less crowded. There is **no documented formulation difference** between the two locations within a single park.

### 2. Flavor Profile Decomposition (sensory targets)
- **Base liquid.**
  - Carbonation: light–moderate, similar to cream soda (lower CO₂ than cola; Jayson's design intent was a beer-like creamy head, so carbonation is intentionally restrained).
  - Sweetness: target **~12–14 °Brix** in the finished base. (Reference: standard US cream soda runs 11–13 °Bx, and Jayson noted in *Bon Appétit* that a 12-oz Butterbeer has *fewer* calories than a 12-oz can of soda — implying the finished drink is in the same Brix neighborhood as cream soda, not a syrup-loaded mixer.)
  - Body/viscosity: only marginally above plain cream soda; not syrupy.
  - Color: medium amber/gold, achieved by caramel color in commercial cream sodas or by the natural color of homemade brown-sugar butterscotch syrup.
- **Foam topping.**
  - Texture: cross between marshmallow fluff and whipped topping — soft-peaked, holds a sculpted dollop for 5–10 minutes at room temperature, melts gradually into the drink rather than collapsing.
  - Flavor: butterscotch-forward with butter and shortbread/vanilla notes; less rich than a butter-based Chantilly.
  - Melt rate: ~25% volume loss over 10 minutes at 70 °F is typical.
  - Structural stability: Dream Whip + marshmallow creme combination holds shape much longer than a dairy Chantilly because of the marshmallow's protein–sugar matrix and the topping mix's emulsifiers.
- **Aromatics, ranked.** (1) butterscotch (dominant), (2) vanilla / cream-soda, (3) butter, (4) caramel/toffee, (5) shortbread (a perceptual blend of butter + vanilla + a hint of salt), (6) faint rum-like note from caramelized brown sugar.
- **Mouthfeel.** Light carbonation cut by a pillow of fatty foam at the lip line. The salt in the foam cuts the perceived sweetness sharply — guests routinely describe the finish as "sweet at first sip, salty/butterscotch on the swallow." Finish length is medium-long, lingering as a buttered-toffee aftertaste.

## Details

### 4. Ingredient Engineering

**Cream soda base (rank-ordered).**
- **Best:** IBC Cream Soda (real cane sugar, glass-bottled, vanilla-forward), or A&W Cream Soda (rounder/mellower per fan-blogger consensus, also widely available). Avoid root beer — it pulls the flavor into wintergreen/sassafras territory, which is wrong.
- **Acceptable swap:** Reed's Flying Cauldron Butterscotch Beer (already butterscotch-forward — reduce added butterscotch syrup by half if you use this).
- **DIY base** (if you want HFCS-free control): 1 cup club soda + 1 Tbsp homemade butterscotch syrup + ¼ tsp pure vanilla extract + 1 drop yellow food coloring. This trades carbonation level for ingredient control.

**Butterscotch element — fresh-batch homemade syrup (the spec's preferred approach).**
- Ratio: **4 Tbsp unsalted butter : ¾ cup dark brown sugar : ¾ cup heavy cream : ½ tsp kosher salt : 1 tsp vanilla extract.** (This is the standard butterscotch ratio used by The Kitchn, Just a Taste, and Ashlee Marie, and tuned for this application by erring slightly toward dark brown sugar for molasses depth.)
- Method: melt butter, add sugar and salt, cook 3–5 min until smooth; whisk in cream off heat (avoid violent boil-over), return to medium-low and cook to **225 °F** on a candy thermometer; off heat, stir in vanilla. Cool to lukewarm.
- Yield: ~1¼ cups syrup; enough for ~10 servings.
- **Refrigerator life: 3–5 days fresh-tasting in a sealed jar** (food-safe up to 4 weeks but loses brightness after a week). Re-warm 10 sec at 50% power in microwave to re-pour.
- **Why fresh, not commercial:** Torani, DaVinci, and Monin butterscotch syrups are sucrose-based with artificial flavor and lack the brown butter/molasses caramelization that defines real butterscotch. Smucker's butterscotch ice-cream topping (commonly recommended in copycat recipes) is the best shelf-stable shortcut; Mrs. Richardson's Butterscotch Caramel is also acceptable. But for the recipe spec's stated quality bar, the homemade fresh syrup wins on every taste-test we located.

**Foam — the canonical "butter foam" (recommended formulation).**
After weighing the published reverse-engineerings against Universal's allergen sheet, the **Dream Whip + marshmallow creme + butterscotch + extracts** approach is recommended:
- ½ envelope (≈1 Tbsp powder) Dream Whip whipped topping mix (or equivalent: Rich's Whip Topping). Provides structure and the whey/sodium-caseinate matrix that matches park foam.
- ¼ cup marshmallow creme (Marshmallow Fluff or Jet-Puffed). Provides the "marshmallowy" persistence.
- 2 Tbsp very hot water (to soften the marshmallow creme).
- 2 Tbsp powdered sugar.
- ¼ tsp fine salt.
- 2 tsp homemade butterscotch syrup (or Smucker's butterscotch).
- ¼ tsp butter extract (Watkins recommended — clean, no artificial harshness).
- ⅛ tsp caramel extract.
- ¼ tsp pure vanilla extract.

This produces a foam that:
- Holds a piped/dolloped peak for 8–12 minutes at room temperature.
- Tastes butterscotch/butter/vanilla without being cloying.
- Matches the Universal foam's slight tackiness (the marshmallow component) better than any pure heavy-cream Chantilly.

**Why not pure heavy cream?** Heavy-cream Chantilly with butterscotch is what most casual copycats use. It tastes great but it (a) collapses noticeably faster on a chilled mug (5–7 min), (b) feels too rich/dairy on the tongue compared to the park version, and (c) doesn't match the whey-bearing-but-non-dairy-base architecture documented on Universal's allergen sheet. Use the dairy version only if you're willing to sacrifice authenticity for ingredient simplicity — it does work better on the *hot* variant (see below).

**Salt.** Target ~⅛ tsp dissolved in the base per 12 oz, plus another ¼ tsp in the foam batch. Salt's role is **critical for cutting sweetness** — many home reports of "way too sweet" are diagnostic of under-salted recipes. The park version is famously perceived as "salty as you swallow" (per a guest interview quoted by NBC News in June 2010), and that's the salt working.

**Optional dairy in the base.** Some clones add 1–2 Tbsp heavy cream to the cold base. **Don't.** Universal's base is dairy-free by design, and adding cream pushes the drink toward a milkshake. The foam supplies enough fat-on-the-palate.

### 5. Three Variants — Tuned Formulations (one serving = 12 oz mug)

#### Cold (Original) Butterbeer

**Base (per 12-oz serving):**
- 10 oz IBC or A&W cream soda, well-chilled (or 8 oz cream soda + 2 oz club soda for a less-sweet base)
- 2 Tbsp fresh homemade butterscotch syrup, lukewarm-to-room-temp
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
- ¼ tsp pure vanilla extract
- ⅛ tsp butter extract (optional but recommended for "shortbread" lift)

**Foam (per 4 servings — store extra in fridge up to 24 h):**
Recipe as above (Dream Whip + marshmallow creme + butterscotch + extracts).

**Procedure:**
1. Make foam first. Stir hot water and marshmallow creme until smooth. Chill 20–25 min in freezer until just cool. Add Dream Whip powder, powdered sugar, salt; whip 60–90 sec to soft peaks. Fold in butterscotch syrup and extracts.
2. In each chilled mug, stir 2 Tbsp butterscotch syrup into 2 oz of the cream soda until fully dissolved. Add salt and extracts. Slowly pour remaining 8 oz cream soda down the side of the mug to preserve carbonation.
3. Spoon or pipe a generous dollop of foam (~⅓ cup, fluffed). Don't stir.
4. Serve immediately. **Optimal sip-window: 0–8 minutes.**

#### Frozen Butterbeer

**Base (yields ~4 cups, serves 4):**
- 24 oz cream soda (2 cans/bottles), well-chilled
- ½ cup fresh homemade butterscotch syrup
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- ¼ tsp butter extract
- ¼ tsp caramel extract (optional)

**Method (best to worst):**
1. **Best texture: Ninja Slushi or commercial slushy machine.** Pour combined base into machine, run "Slush" cycle 25–30 min. Aim for soft-serve thick, not solid. The dissolved sugar (target 18–22 °Bx) keeps ice crystals small.
2. **Excellent: Ice-cream maker.** Pour combined base into pre-frozen Cuisinart-style canister, churn 20–25 min until slushy.
3. **Good: Stir-freeze method.** Combine base in metal pan, freeze 30 min, stir vigorously with a fork to break crystals, repeat every 30 min for 2–3 hours.
4. **Acceptable: Blender method.** Freeze 12 oz cream soda in ice-cube trays overnight; blend frozen cubes with remaining 12 oz cream soda + butterscotch syrup + extracts until slushy. Add 1 Tbsp powdered sugar if texture is too icy (more sugar = smaller crystals).

**Avoid:** Adding plain water-ice to fresh base. It dilutes flavor and produces grainy texture.

**Top with foam as for Cold.** Frozen Butterbeer's foam holds longer because the cold base slows melting. Sip-window: 0–15 min, melting gracefully.

**Ice-crystal control note:** A frozen base needs Brix of ~18–22 to scoop without freezing solid. Below 14 Brix, you'll get an ice block; above 25, it won't freeze enough to hold form. If you're using a less-sweet cream soda or club-soda base, add 1–2 Tbsp simple syrup to land in range.

#### Hot Butterbeer

**Note on park version:** Universal's Hot Butterbeer is **not** simply the cold drink heated. It's a separate, dairy-based formulation (whole milk, butter, brown sugar, butterscotch flavor, foam topping) that more closely resembles a butterscotch latte. It also is **not** offered foam-free (the dairy is in the base, not just the topping). This is why it has a richer "nutty hot chocolate" character than cold.

**Base (per 12-oz serving):**
- 1 cup whole milk (or oat milk for dairy-free)
- 2 Tbsp unsalted butter
- 2 Tbsp packed dark brown sugar
- 2 Tbsp fresh homemade butterscotch syrup
- 2 Tbsp heavy cream (richens; can swap for additional milk)
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
- ½ tsp pure vanilla extract
- ¼ tsp butter extract
- 2 Tbsp cream soda, finishing splash (optional, adds a faint cream-soda lift)

**Procedure:**
1. In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, combine milk, butter, brown sugar, and salt. Whisk until butter is melted and sugar dissolves, ~3–4 min. Do not boil.
2. Whisk in butterscotch syrup and heavy cream. Heat to **160–165 °F** (steaming, not bubbling). Off heat, stir in vanilla and butter extract.
3. Pour into a warmed mug. Add the cream soda splash if using.
4. Top with the same foam as cold (or, alternatively, lightly whipped sweetened heavy cream — for hot, the dairy Chantilly works better because the contrast vs. a hot beverage helps the foam survive).

Sip-window: 0–10 min while still warm.

### 6. Process & Equipment

**Minimum kitchen.** A medium saucepan, a candy thermometer (or instant-read), a hand whisk or hand mixer, and a chilled mug.

**Optional upgrades.**
- **iSi cream whipper** (0.5 L): Charge the foam mixture (loaded into the canister with one N₂O charger after the marshmallow has cooled to fridge temperature) and dispense — produces a finer, more uniform foam that holds 15+ min.
- **Soda siphon** (2 L): Lets you carbonate a homemade base (water + butterscotch syrup + vanilla) for absolute control over CO₂ and sweetness, useful if commercial cream sodas are too sweet for your palate.
- **Immersion blender:** Use only at the foam-making stage (folding marshmallow creme into Dream Whip) — never on the carbonated base, which will go flat instantly.
- **Refractometer (optional, $20–40):** Lets you actually hit the 12–14 °Bx target on the base and 18–22 °Bx on the frozen base.

**Make-ahead and storage.**
- **Butterscotch syrup:** sealed jar, refrigerator, 3–5 days for peak flavor / up to 4 weeks for safety. Re-warm before each use.
- **Foam:** best within 1 hour of whipping. Refrigerated, will keep 24 hours but will need a brief re-whip. Don't freeze — it breaks.
- **Cold base:** mix syrup + extracts ahead and refrigerate, but **add cream soda immediately before serving** or carbonation is lost.
- **Frozen base:** can hold in freezer up to a week; re-blend or re-churn briefly before serving to restore slush texture.
- **Hot base:** can be made up to 1 day ahead (without cream soda) and gently re-warmed.

### 7. Calibration & Testing

**Sweetness/Brix dial-in.**
- Make a 12-oz reference using 10 oz cream soda + 2 Tbsp butterscotch syrup. Taste alongside (a) +1 Tbsp syrup version and (b) –1 Tbsp syrup version. The park version is closer to "less sweet" than most copycats; if your reference tastes "exactly like Universal," you've got it. If you like it sweeter, go +½ Tbsp syrup; if you find it cloying, dilute 1 oz of the cream soda with 1 oz club soda.
- For frozen, the freezer concentrates perceived sweetness — back off butterscotch by ~15% relative to cold.

**Butterscotch intensity dial-in.**
- The park version sits in the "butterscotch is dominant but not single-note" range. If your foam tastes too much like marshmallow, increase butterscotch in the foam by ½ tsp at a time. If the base tastes like flat cream soda, increase butter extract by drops (extracts at this intensity are over-additive — start with 2 drops at a time).

**Common failure modes and fixes.**
- **Foam collapses in 2–3 minutes.** Causes: (1) used heavy cream alone — switch to Dream Whip + marshmallow architecture; (2) whipped past stiff peaks — re-whip a fresh batch only to soft peaks; (3) mug was too warm.
- **Foam tastes cloyingly sweet.** Increase salt in foam to ½ tsp; decrease powdered sugar to 1 Tbsp.
- **Grainy butterscotch syrup.** Caused by sugar crystallizing on cold cream addition or by overcooking. Fix by gently re-warming with 1 Tbsp water and whisking; ensure you bring to exactly 225 °F next time, no higher.
- **Base too sweet.** Cut cream soda 25% with club soda and re-balance butterscotch.
- **Base loses fizz.** Always pour soda last and slowly, down the side of the mug; don't whisk after pouring.
- **Frozen base freezes solid.** Brix too low — add 1–2 Tbsp simple syrup or another tablespoon of butterscotch syrup before re-freezing.

**Substitutions for dietary restrictions.**
- **Vegan / fully dairy-free:** Replace Dream Whip with a coconut-cream-based whipped topping (chilled coconut cream whipped with 2 Tbsp powdered sugar). Use vegan butterscotch syrup made with vegan butter and full-fat coconut milk in place of butter and heavy cream — the Healthy Slow Cooking version is the strongest published recipe. The cold/frozen base is naturally vegan if you check your cream soda label (most are).
- **Gluten-free:** Naturally GF if you skip the malt-based imitation cream sodas (rare in US market). Cream soda, butterscotch syrup, and Dream Whip are all GF; Marshmallow Fluff is GF.
- **Lower-sugar:** Use Zevia Cream Soda (stevia-sweetened) plus a sugar-free butterscotch syrup made with allulose or erythritol. Net-carb impact: ~75% reduction. Flavor takes a small hit; the brown-sugar caramelization is part of what makes butterscotch taste like butterscotch, and sugar substitutes don't caramelize the same way.
- **Lower-fat:** Reduce heavy cream in butterscotch syrup to ½ cup; substitute 2% milk in hot version. Foam can be made with skim Dream Whip preparation (per package).

## Recommendations (Decision-Ready)

1. **Make the homemade butterscotch syrup first** (5 min active, 15 min total). It is the single highest-leverage component and elevates every variant. Don't substitute Torani or DaVinci unless you are time-constrained.
2. **Adopt the Dream Whip + marshmallow creme + extracts foam.** It's the closest match to Universal's actual foam architecture and it holds shape longer than any heavy-cream alternative.
3. **For the cold variant, use IBC Cream Soda over A&W** if available; IBC is bottled, real-cane-sugar-sweetened, and has the cleanest vanilla-forward profile.
4. **For the frozen variant, invest 25 minutes in either an ice-cream maker or a Ninja Slushi.** Blender-with-ice methods produce noticeably grainier results in side-by-side tastings.
5. **For the hot variant, treat it as a separate recipe** — do not attempt to heat the cold formulation. The dairy base + steamed butter + brown sugar approach replicates Universal's nutty/latte-like character far better.
6. **Salt is non-negotiable.** Skip it and your drink will taste like one-note sugar. Aim for ⅛ tsp in base + ¼ tsp in a 4-serving foam batch as a starting point.
7. **Benchmarks for adjustment:**
   - If guests describe it as "too sweet" → reduce butterscotch syrup 25%; increase salt 25%.
   - If they say it lacks butterscotch character → increase butter extract 1 drop at a time; do not first reach for more butterscotch syrup (which adds sugar).
   - If foam doesn't hold past 5 min → switch to the Dream Whip architecture and chill the marshmallow stage longer.
   - If you have a refractometer and want a quantitative pass: hit 12–14 °Bx for the cold base and 18–22 °Bx for the frozen base; adjust syrup up or down accordingly.

## Caveats

- **The exact Universal recipe is a trade secret.** Every ingredient quantity in this document is a best-judgment formulation derived from triangulating (a) Steve Jayson's published flavor-intent quotes in *Bon Appétit* and *Marketplace*, (b) the documented Universal allergen sheet, (c) the Reed's Flying Cauldron commercial proxy, and (d) the consensus of multiple side-by-side fan reverse-engineerings. It is not the actual Universal recipe.
- **Foam architecture confidence.** "High" — the whey-containing-non-dairy-base structure is multiply attested in Universal's own allergen disclosures. The exact emulsifier system Universal uses (likely a commercial whip-topping mix similar to Rich's Whip) is not public.
- **Bon Appétit January 2002 quote.** The "less-sickly butterscotch" Rowling quote is firmly attested in the Harry Potter Lexicon and AP wire reporting, but Condé Nast does not host pre-2003 *BA* issues online; the original print page could not be directly verified.
- **Hot Butterbeer formulation confidence.** "Medium" — Universal has confirmed that the hot version's base contains dairy, but the precise milk/cream/butter ratio is not published. The recipe here is a consensus best-practice.
- **Speculation flags.** Statements about the *exact* commercial whip-topping brand Universal uses, the *exact* sugar Brix of the park drink, and the *exact* plant base of the post-2 March 2023 vegan foam are not publicly disclosed and should be treated as approximations.
- **The "Yes, Chef" anecdote** originates from Jim Hill's 2011 reporting of Jayson's own retelling. Rowling has not, to my knowledge, publicly confirmed the exact wording.
- **Recipe blogs vary widely in rigor.** Many "copycat butterbeer" recipes online use heavy whipping cream + butterscotch syrup; this is a reasonable home approximation but does not match the documented Universal architecture. The recipes by Todd Wilbur (Top Secret Recipes), Ashlee Marie, Taste of Home, and BabyWise Mom are the most rigorously developed of the bunch.
